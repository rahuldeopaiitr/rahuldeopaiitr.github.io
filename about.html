Biography

I am a PhD Research Scholar in the Department of Water Resources Development and Management at IIT Roorkee, working at the intersection of hydrology, flood hydrodynamics, water quality, and public-health risk. My research addresses a question conventional flood assessments often overlook: not only where floodwater spreads, but what contaminants it carries, how people are exposed, and what health risks may result.

My doctoral research quantifies public-health risks associated with contaminated floodwaters in flood-prone metropolitan regions — examining how flooding mobilises and transports microbial contamination, and translating these dynamics into spatially explicit estimates of human exposure and infection risk under present and future climatic conditions.

As part of this, I developed HyEco, a hybrid hydrodynamic–ecological modelling framework integrating flood inundation modelling (MIKE+, MIKE FLOOD), microbial fate-and-transport simulation (MIKE ECO Lab), and Quantitative Microbial Risk Assessment (QMRA) — linking flood behaviour, contaminant transport, and population exposure to support health-risk assessment and informed urban flood management.

Beyond flood-borne health risk, I work on hydroclimatic extremes — design-rainfall regionalisation for data-scarce catchments, statistical downscaling, and satellite-based hydroclimatic assessment — building customised analytical workflows in Python and R. I welcome interdisciplinary collaborations on urban flood risk, water quality, climate adaptation, and environmental-health linkages.
